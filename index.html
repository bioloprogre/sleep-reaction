Sleep & Reaction

Step 1 of 4 · Information & consent

Does last night's sleep affect your reaction time?

For my biology investigation I am measuring whether the amount of sleep you got last night is related to how fast you react. You answer four short questions and then do a reaction test. It takes about 3 minutes.

  • You can take part if you are between 16 and 25 years old.
  • Taking part is voluntary and anonymous. I do not ask for your name or any contact details.
  • You can stop at any time. Nothing is saved until the very last step.
  • At the end you get a participant ID. Send it to me if you want your data deleted later.
  • The data is only used for this school project.

Step 3 of 4 · How the test works

React as soon as the screen turns yellow

Wait …
NOW!
  • The screen is dark grey. After a random delay it turns yellow.
  • As soon as it turns yellow, click or tap anywhere on it as fast as you can.
  • If you press too early, that counts as a false start and the trial is repeated.
  • You start with 3 practice trials that do not count, followed by 10 recorded trials.

Please sit still, turn off music and notifications, and use the same hand and the same device for the whole test.

Practice finished

Now for the 10 recorded trials

Same as before: as soon as the screen turns yellow, click or tap as fast as you can.

Exclusion criteria

A trial is valid if it lies between 100 and 1000 ms. Values below 100 ms are anticipations, values above 1000 ms suggest distraction. Each participant is represented by the median of their valid trials.
